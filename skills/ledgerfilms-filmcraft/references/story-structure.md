# Story building — the LedgerFilms documentary spine

Business documentaries fail as stories when they list events. They work when a character/system with a goal meets an obstacle whose mechanism the audience watches operate.

## Topic fidelity (owner's rule, 2026-11-03)
The story follows the topic — not a genre template, not whatever footage is easy to generate. Before beats are written, the beat sheet states the topic's central question in one sentence (the Angle from intake). Every beat below must answer, complicate or pay off that question; a beat that could be transplanted into a different company's video unchanged is off-topic and is cut at the sheet stage, where cutting is cheap.

## Beat sheet (≈10 min, ~1,400–1,600 words)
1. **Cold-open hook (0:00–0:30)** — the contradiction in one image + one line (“record quarter; worst day in its history”). No logos, no throat-clearing. Ends on an unanswered question.
2. **The world before (0:30–2:00)** — origin told as scenes, not biography: a place, a person, a small first version of the machine. Establish the visual motif here.
3. **The mechanism (2:00–4:00)** — how the money actually moves, shown step by step (the “two doors”). This is the channel's promise: the viewer must be able to redraw it afterwards.
4. **Escalation (4:00–6:00)** — the machine works too well; dependency quietly grows; numbers climb on screen as graphics tied to lines.
5. **The turn (6:00–7:30)** — one dated event reverses the direction. Shot in a location/prop the viewer already knows, changed.
6. **Consequences (7:30–9:00)** — visible fallout in the established world: emptier hall, closed door, new behaviour. Stakes stated in human terms.
7. **The lesson + loop (9:00–10:00)** — one sentence the viewer can repeat tomorrow (“the contract is not the relationship”), then a tease that opens the next video's question.

## Character & stakes
Even a company story needs a human carrier: a founder figure, a user, a shopkeeper — symbolic, consistent, with a want. Stakes are always translated: not “DAUq fell” but “the hall emptied.” Numbers appear as evidence for the drama, never instead of it.

## Tension devices
- **Dramatic irony:** the audience is shown the dependency before the characters price it in.
- **The clock:** dated events; “in October” becomes a visible counter draining.
- **The repeated shot:** same framing at hook/turn/payoff, changed each time — the cheapest, strongest continuity device in AI film.
- **Withheld answer:** the hook's question is only fully answered in the lesson.

## Kill list (story-killers seen in rejected work)
Chronology without causality (“and then, and then”); narration summarising what no shot shows; three locations with no recurring place; an ending that restates the intro instead of paying it off.

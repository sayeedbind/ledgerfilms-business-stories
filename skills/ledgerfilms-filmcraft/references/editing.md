# Editing — professional assembly standard

Editing is where the screenplay becomes cinema or collapses into a slideshow. These rules are applied as a formal pass on the assembled timeline before export (see the EDIT PASS command at the bottom). Owner's directives, 2026-11-03: the story follows the topic; cinematography follows the script and voiceover; editing is professional; the film is multi-angle cinema — never a single zoom-in/zoom-out style.

## Governing principles
1. **Story follows the topic.** Every sequence must visibly advance the topic's central question (stated in the beat sheet). A beautiful sequence that doesn't serve the topic is cut or never generated.
2. **Cinematography follows the script & voiceover.** Camera size, angle and movement are chosen FROM the screenplay row they serve — the [CAMERA] block justifies itself against that row's AUDIO line. No decorative camera work the narration didn't call for.
3. **Cut on the idea.** A cut lands where the narration's idea changes, on a completed action, or on a deliberate visual rhyme (match on the motif). Never mid-gesture unless the next shot completes the same gesture from a new angle (cutting on action).

## Multi-angle cinema (the anti-zoom rule)
- **No zoom-style film.** Zoom In editor filter, Fast Cut transitions and push-in-on-every-clip are banned. A zoom/push is allowed only as a single motivated choice inside one shot (realisation beat), never as the film's default motion.
- **Every sequence (one location/idea) is covered from at least 3 distinct angles or sizes** — e.g. establishing WS → MS action → CU/ECU detail or insert → release. If the pack only has one angle for a sequence, the pack is wrong, not the edit.
- **No two adjacent shots share size + angle.** Change size, angle (≥30°), or subject at every cut.
- **Movement variety budget:** across any 10 consecutive shots, use at least 4 different camera behaviours (locked-off, push-in, pull-back, track/follow, pan, tilt, crane, handheld). All-push-in or all-static both fail.
- **Screen direction and eyelines** stay consistent within a space; crossings happen on a moving shot or a neutral (head-on/top-down) shot.

## Rhythm & sound
- Shot length follows the narration beat (PLAYBOOK §6/v1.7 scene-length rule); the average tightens at the turn, breathes at the lesson. No shot outstays the line it illustrates — trim at the line's end, extend only with the next beat's own shot.
- Narration is continuous because every clip carries its own voiceover; picture cuts happen at clip boundaries under the voice (the visual may lead a cut by a few frames so eye and ear land together). Never trim through a spoken line. Clip production sound stays low under that clip's voiceover; sound bridges (a door slam, a crowd swell) may cross a cut to glue two angles of one space.
- Graphics enter by build/reveal, hold for reading time, exit clean (filmcraft motion-graphics rules). No effect transitions between scenes: clean cuts, with a fade reserved for time-passage or the ending only.

## The EDIT PASS command
When the owner says **“Edit pass”** — or before ANY export — the agent runs this pass on the assembled timeline and reports the result line by line:
1. Topic check: state the topic question; confirm every sequence advances it; name any that doesn't (cut/regenerate list).
2. Angle audit: list size+angle+movement for every shot; flag adjacent repeats, single-angle sequences, movement-variety failures, and any zoom-style motion (remove/replace it).
3. Sync audit: 5 random lines, muted-picture test; picture leads cuts by a few frames; no shot outstays its line.
4. Sound audit: every clip's own voiceover present, locked voice, complete and continuous across cuts; clip production sound low; bridges where used are intentional.
5. Transitions audit: Fast Cut OFF, Zoom In filter OFF, fades only where this standard allows.
6. Coverage gate (PLAYBOOK §9) re-checked after any trim the pass made.
Only when all six pass does the agent save (verified by Open-list timestamp) and export.

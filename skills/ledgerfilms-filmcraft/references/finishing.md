# Finishing — sound, grade, captions, disclosure, retention

The layer between “assembled” and “professional”. Applied after the EDIT PASS, before and after export. Owner-approved addition, 2026-11-03.

## 1. Sound design & music
- **Score is planned in the screenplay, not added in panic.** The beat sheet marks music states per act: sparse under the hook, pulse under the mechanism, rise into the turn, near-silence at the consequence, resolve at the lesson. One licensed/owned track family per video; music never carries lyrics under narration.
- **Designed effects** sit on top of clip production sound: the motif sound (a door, a bell, a drain) recurs at hook/turn/payoff, mixed deliberately — these are placed in the VISUAL column as “SFX: …” so they are budgeted like shots.
- **Mix targets:** narration is king — music and effects duck under it (sidechain-style manual levels in the editor); final loudness ≈ **−14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP** (YouTube normalisation). No silent stretches longer than a deliberate beat (~1.5 s) except the held moment before the lesson.
- **Tool reality:** VideoExpress's audio tools are basic. If the score/mix cannot be achieved there, the finishing step is done in an external audio/video editor on the exported picture — planned in the run sheet, never improvised after export.

## 2. Narration performance (direct the voice)
- The screenplay's AUDIO cells carry performance marks (see screenplay.md): pauses, emphasis, and a **pronunciation list** for names/numbers the TTS is likely to misread — respell inside the spoken text (“Skrip-ting” style) with a plain note outside it; never ship a mispronounced name.
- One locked voice and settings per channel (currently Emotion Andrew Multilingual, speed 1, pitch 0); any change is an owner decision, recorded in the run sheet. Listen to the full narration once, start to finish, before assembly — a flat or wrong read is fixed at the chunk, not covered by music.

## 3. Grade & picture finishing
- **Shot-match the assembled film:** adjacent clips must agree in warmth, contrast and sharpness; the channel grade (muted teal-and-amber, film grain) is the reference frame, and any clip that sits outside it is corrected or regenerated — not averaged away.
- Consistency checks on the final timeline: one frame rate, one resolution, no black/frozen frames (contact-sheet sweep of the finished cut, the same 12-frame method used for takes), graphics inside title-safe areas.

## 4. Captions, chapters, packaging
- **Long-form:** a corrected SRT caption file is part of the upload pack (auto-captions reviewed, names/numbers fixed — captions are a ranking and accessibility asset, not an afterthought). **Shorts:** burned-in captions, channel style, ≤2 lines.
- **Chapters** at the story beats (hook / mechanism / turn / lesson) with viewer-language titles.
- **Packaging triangle:** title, thumbnail and the first 30 seconds must promise the same thing in the same words as the topic question; the thumbnail brief (templates/thumbnail-brief.md) is checked against the finished cold open, not the planned one.

## 5. Compliance at upload (blocking)
- **YouTube AI disclosure:** tick the altered/synthetic content disclosure for realistic AI-generated scenes at upload — every LedgerFilms video, every time; record the tick in the upload pack.
- Sources and attributions from the research brief appear in the description; conditional or single-source claims keep their attribution wording (filmcraft honesty rules apply to text exactly as to graphics).
- No financial advice, no price targets — PLAYBOOK fact rules, restated here because upload is where they are enforced.

## 6. The retention loop (after publish)
- **Before publish, retention is designed:** hook lands in the first 5 seconds; a pattern interrupt (new angle, graphic, question, or sound event) at least every ~20–30 seconds; the lesson is the last new information, not a recap.
- **After publish:** at ~72 hours and ~7 days, read the retention curve and CTR (owner's YouTube Studio). Note: where the curve falls (timestamp + what was on screen), what the hook's first-5-seconds retention was, and one concrete lesson. The lesson is written back into this file or the PLAYBOOK as a dated line — the loop only counts if the next video is made differently because of it.
- A video whose first-5-seconds retention collapses gets its cold open studied against the packaging triangle before any new topic is started.

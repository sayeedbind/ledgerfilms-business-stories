# Titles, thumbnails, metadata

## Titles (long-form)
Formula: **[Familiar name] + [curiosity gap] + [specific stake/number]**
- "The Real Reason Blockbuster Died (It Wasn't Netflix)"
- "How Costco Makes Money From a $1.50 Hot Dog"
- "The Brothers Who Split an Empire: Adidas vs Puma"
Rules: ≤60 characters where possible; the gap must be *answered* in the video (no
unpaid-off clickbait — it kills retention and trust); one number beats three adjectives;
never ALL CAPS for more than one word. Always write 3 options; the agent marks its pick
and why (curiosity + clarity + search term).

## Thumbnails
- **Max 3 words** ("HE SAID NO", "$1.50 FOREVER", "ONE DECISION"), or none.
- One cinematic frame from the video's own scenes (same grade), one face/emotion or one
  symbolic object, huge at mobile size. No charts, no paragraphs, no fake logos.
- Brief template: `templates/thumbnail-brief.md`. Test at 10% zoom: if you can't read it,
  it fails.

## Shorts titles
The hook *is* the title: "Why Costco's hot dog can never cost more" / "Blockbuster had
the chance to buy Netflix — for $50M". Add `#shorts` and link the related long-form in
the Short's pinned comment.

## Description (long-form)
1. Two lines: the question + the answer tease. 2) Chapter timestamps from the story spine.
3) **Sources** list (from the research brief). 4) The not-financial-advice line.
5) One related-video link (end-screen target). Keep keywords natural: company name,
founder name, "how X makes money" / "rise and fall of X" phrasing viewers actually search.

## Pinned comment (every upload)
A question the audience can actually argue about + a fact that didn't fit:
"Blockbuster's late fees were ~16% of revenue in 2000 — would YOU have killed them to
save the company?" Reply to comments for the first 60 minutes after publish.

# Example — LONG-FORM: "The Real Reason Blockbuster Died (It Wasn't Netflix)"

*Format example. Re-verify all facts in a research brief before production.*

**One question:** how did the company that owned Friday night talk itself out of surviving?
**Mechanism to reveal:** late fees and store economics made the *profitable* choice the
*fatal* one — the business model itself blocked every rescue.
**Pillar:** Rise & Fall · **Protagonist:** Blockbuster (desire: protect store profits ·
blind spot: the customer experience it was monetising).

## Outline on the spine
- **Cold open:** 2000 — Netflix's founders offer to sell for $50 million (widely reported
  figure; verify against two sources) — and get laughed out of the room. "This is the story
  of why saying no felt *rational*."
- **Stakes:** by the mid-2000s Blockbuster was a multi-billion-dollar giant with ~9,000
  stores at its peak (verify year/source in brief).
- **Origin:** 1985, Dallas — a software man's data-driven video store (founder David Cook).
- **Mechanism:** rental economics — new-release scarcity, weekend peaks, and late fees as a
  profit pillar (reported at roughly 16% of revenue around 2000 — verify).
- **Re-hooks:** the Netflix offer (again, with the numbers) · the debt-loaded Viacom
  spin-off · "Total Access" actually working — then being strangled internally.
- **Turning point:** leadership change; the online programme is cut to protect stores.
- **Climax:** 2010 bankruptcy; stores liquidated; one franchised store in Bend, Oregon remains.
- **Lesson:** when your profit comes from a friction your customers hate, a competitor's
  first product is removing it. (Observation, not advice.)
- **Outro line:** "Costco built an empire doing the exact opposite — that story is next."

## Bible excerpt
- **Style line:** channel standard (see bible template).
- **Era blocks:** 1985 (CRT TVs, VHS shelves, neon signage, no mobile phones) ·
  2000 (DVD transition, flat CRTs, early broadband office) · 2010 (empty aisles, sale banners).
- **Location block — 1990s flagship store:** `rows of blue-and-yellow shelving (colour
  shapes only, no readable text), new-release wall at the back, counter left of the door,
  camera from the entrance side.` Key counts: one counter, one new-release wall.
- **Motifs:** a yellow rental card · Friday-night queue · the "be kind, rewind" era sticker
  as abstract shape (no readable text in frame).

# Example — SHORT: "Why Costco's Hot Dog Can Never Cost More"

*Format example — a complete Short. Re-verify facts before production: the $1.50 price has
held since the mid-1980s (widely reported); the famous founder exchange is anecdote —
narrate it as "the story goes", never as a transcript.*

**One fact:** the price hasn't moved in ~40 years while everything around it inflated.
**Reveal (≈70%):** the hot dog isn't a product — it's a *promise* that keeps memberships renewing.

## Script (145 words)
> This hot dog has cost one dollar fifty since the nineteen eighties.
> Since then, almost everything else has roughly tripled in price.
> Costco sells millions of them every year — and loses money on nearly every one.
> So why not raise the price by fifty cents? Nobody would even notice.
> Because the hot dog was never the business.
> The warehouse is.
> Costco makes its money from membership fees — and members renew because they trust
> Costco is on their side.
> One famous story says a Costco president suggested raising the price — and was told,
> in blunt language, that the price would not change while the founder was alive.
> The hot dog is a receipt. Proof, in your hand, that the promise still holds.
> One dollar fifty. The cheapest trust exercise in retail.
> The full story of how Costco makes money is on the channel.
